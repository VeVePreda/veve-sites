The **Star Wars: Death Trooper Movie Props** series has produced the steepest floor
increases we have recorded this month. Three of its pieces sit at the top of our
seven-day movers, and the pattern is unusually consistent across the set.

## What the records show

Unlike a single spike driven by one listing, the increase here appears across
several items of the same series within the same window. When an entire set moves
together, it usually points to renewed collector interest rather than a thin order
book — a distinction that matters when you are reading a floor price.

## Reading a floor price honestly

A floor is the cheapest asking price, not a market value. It moves when one seller
relists, and it can fall back just as fast. This is why we only display a percentage
change when an item has enough recorded history behind it: a variation computed on
two data points tells you nothing.

## What to watch next

The number of listings matters as much as the price. A rising floor with shrinking
supply is a very different signal from a rising floor with stable supply.
